Understanding the Risks

Below you’ll find a full explanation of the risks associated with the things you do and share online. Not all actions are equally risky — context matters, so where you post something can make a big difference. For example, sharing your location privately with friends is very different from posting it publicly on a livestream. That’s why we’ve broken things down into separate tables based on the type of platform you’re using.

Check out the links below to explore each category in more detail:

  • Activities done online
  • Information shared on social media platforms
  • Information shared on online shopping and services platforms
  • Information shared on school or government platforms
  • Information shared on gaming platforms

The more you understand, the more in control you’ll be of your data footprint. Stay safe out there!

Activites Done Online

Activity Risk Level Why it Might Be Risky
Lurking / reading Low Passive activity; no data is shared.
Liking / reacting Low Minimal data is shared; patterns may still be tracked.
Following accounts / subscribing Low May reveal interests, but limited direct exposure.
Sharing links to external content Medium Can indicate views and interests; can include tracking links.
Commenting Medium Reveals opinions; may include personal info unintentionally.
Posting content Medium May contain identifying details or patterns of behaviour.
Messaging (private chats/DMs) Medium Private, but still stored and potentially exposed.
Creating/joining public groups/events Medium Shows interests, affiliations, or physical presence.
Participating in forums Medium Often permanent, searchable, and potentially identifying.
Uploading videos High Can reveal appearance, voice, surroundings, identity.
Syncing contacts High Shares your personal network with the platform.
Livestreaming Very High Real-time exposure with high potential for oversharing.
Using location-based apps Very High Tracks movements and habits in real time.
Tagging people or places in posts Very High Reveals identity, location, and relationships.

Information Shared on Social Media Platforms

Type of Info Shared Sensitivity Level Why it Might Be Risky
Nickname/first name only Low Low risk of being personally identified or tracked.
Full name Medium Easier for strangers to find you and other personal details about you.
Email address Medium Can be used for tracking, spam, or phishing attempts.
Date of birth Medium Used in security questions. May expose you to identity theft risks.
Opinions or beliefs (e.g. politics) Medium Can be used to profile or target you with manipulative content or ads.
School / workplace High Reveals daily routine and location, increasing risk of unwanted contact or stalking.
Phone number High Used for verification, but also enables spam, scams, tracking, and potential account takeover.
Home address High Reveals where you live; risk of stalking or physical harm.
Browsing history (via linked accounts) High Can build an in-depth profile of your habits and interests which could be used for manipulation.
Third-party app permissions High Allows other apps access to your data, often with little transparency.
Photos with faces High Risks include facial recognition abuse and unwanted identification.
Videos with voice/image High Reveals multiple personal characteristics and details, increasing privacy invasion risk.
Real-time location Very High Can be used to track movements and current position, increasing risk of stalking or physical harm.
Identity documents (ID/passport) Very High Highly sensitive; can lead to identity theft or fraud.
